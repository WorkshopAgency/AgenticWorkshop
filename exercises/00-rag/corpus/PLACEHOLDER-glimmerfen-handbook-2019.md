## About the station

The Glimmerfen Moss Station is a fictional field station on the island of
Ollerby, founded in 1987 by the botanist Hesper Quill. It studies the
luminous bog moss *Sphagnum lucidum fictum*, which glows faintly green on
nights after heavy rain. The station has four buildings: the Lamp House, the
drying shed, the bunk house and the boat store.

## Sampling rules

Moss cores are taken with the brass corer kept in the Lamp House. Each core is
exactly 14 centimetres deep. Volunteers must log every core in the blue ledger
before supper. Cores are dried for 36 hours in the drying shed at 40 degrees
Celsius before weighing.

The station counts glowing moss patches along the Eastern Transect, which is
1.2 kilometres long and marked by 31 white posts. Counts start one hour after
sunset and must be finished within ninety minutes.

## Safety

Visitors may not enter the bog alone. The minimum group size on the bog is
two people, and one of them must carry the orange radio. The boat to the
mainland leaves on Tuesdays and Fridays at 07:30.
